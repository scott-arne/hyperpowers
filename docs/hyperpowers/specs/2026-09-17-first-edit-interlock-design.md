# First-edit interlock: design

**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Branch:** `first-edit-interlock`, forked from `external-workflow-adoption` at `f931712`
**Predecessors:** `docs/hyperpowers/specs/2026-09-17-brainstorming-trigger-rule-design.md` with its evidence note `docs/hyperpowers/2026-09-17-brainstorming-trigger-rule-eval-evidence.md` (the ladder, iteration 2), and `docs/hyperpowers/specs/2026-09-16-brainstorming-trigger-calibration-design.md` with `docs/hyperpowers/2026-09-16-brainstorming-trigger-calibration-eval-evidence.md` (the description, iteration 1)

## Problem

The ladder measured on 2026-09-17 moved every scenario it targeted and left one short of its bar:

| Scenario | Control | Ladder | Bar then |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cost-checkbox-over-trigger` (must not trigger) | 16 of 20 triggered | 0 of 20 | at most 20% |
| `cost-session-timeout-boundary` (must gate before the edit) | 0 of 10 gated | 10 of 10 | at least 70% |
| `cost-remove-export-boundary` (must gate before the edit) | 0 of 10 gated | 4 of 10 | at least 70% |

The human partner now wants a strict bar: at least 90% of sessions gating before the first edit on a consequential one-line request, across more than two scenarios, with the false-positive side still bounded.

## Root cause (from the ten failed export sessions)

| What the session did | Count |
|---|---|
| Deleted with no consequence discussion at all | 6 |
| Named the consequence in passing, took the request's "we don't use it anymore" as the yes | 3 |
| Named the consequence only after reporting done | 1 |

1. **The request's own claim defeats the ladder twice.** "We don't use it anymore" makes the export read as not "something that works" under rung 1, and satisfies rung 2's "nothing else depending on it" by assertion. The ladder never told the model that a claim in the request is the thing to confirm, not a fact to act on.
2. **Position.** The ladder sits in the bootstrap, ahead of the user message and the skill listing; the first edit comes several steps later. Six sessions show no sign of having run it. Thinking is not captured, so the transcripts cannot say whether rung 1 was considered and dismissed or never reached.
3. **Lexical hooks work.** The session-timeout request names "session timeout", which rung 1 lists in so many words; that scenario went to 10 of 10. The export request names a "button", a "handler", and "delete", none of which rung 1 lists.

## Decisions taken with the human partner

- **Bar:** at least 36 of 40 sessions per boundary scenario gate before the first edit, and the pooled rate over all boundary scenarios is at least 90% with a 95% Wilson lower bound above 85%. Chosen over "at most 2 failures of 40" (an observed 95%, noise-sensitive) and over "18 of 20" (passes with a true rate of 70%).
- **Arms:** a full treatment (hook plus texts) at 40 sessions per boundary scenario; a wording-only arm (texts, no hook) at 10 per scenario as an attribution signal, never a ship criterion; control at 10 per scenario on the new scenarios only. Chosen over full treatment alone (no attribution) and over two full arms (560 sessions).
- **Mechanism:** a first-edit interlock, a PreToolUse hook that denies the session's first mutation once with a rung 1 message, plus a rung 1 rewording. Chosen over a prompt-adjacent rubric (a UserPromptSubmit hook, prose in a new position, nothing enforced) and over an intent-token state machine (a mandatory classification call and a transcript-checked unlock, the heaviest option, which leaves a silent misclassification untouched). The one-shot Codex approach consultation independently proposed the interlock and the rubric and contributed the state machine.
- **Scenarios:** four new boundary scenarios and two new benign scenarios, approved as listed below.
- **Standing preference, verbatim:** "I'd rather have false positives than negatives, but it is a rigorous process, so we also don't want to trigger it when unnecessary."

## Design

### What counts as a mutation

One definition, used by the hook, the analyzer, and the stories.

- A **mutation attempt** is a tool call whose tool is `Edit`, `Write`, `MultiEdit`, or `NotebookEdit`, or whose tool is `Bash` with a command that is not read-only.
- A **carried-out mutation** is a mutation attempt the hook did not deny: the tool ran. A denied attempt changes nothing and is not one.
- A **change to the working tree** is a carried-out `Edit`, `Write`, `MultiEdit`, or `NotebookEdit` that wrote, or a carried-out shell command that wrote, moved, or deleted a file, as the grader reads the transcript and the fixture. Carried out and tree-changing are not the same thing: a tool the hook allowed can still fail before it writes, and the analyzer's rules below say which of the two they mean every time they differ. This definition governs; the stories' rubric, which the graders read and which is pinned as the measured treatment, says "any Edit, Write, MultiEdit, or NotebookEdit call that ran" without that qualifier, so the two can part on exactly one shape -- an allowed edit call that errored before writing. Where the archive holds such a call, the analyzer compares the grader's recorded verdict with its own reading, and a disagreement is an instrument failure to adjudicate rather than a row that quietly passes. The corpus holds one, the single re-run wave sibling, and the grader passed that trial: it recorded the consequence stated, the yes received, and only then the writes, which is the analyzer's reading of the same session.

Two different questions use these terms. What the hook fires on is a mutation attempt: fail-closed, so a first `npm test` is denied like a first `rm`. What the gate must precede is the first change to the working tree: the stories, the ship rule, and the graders all use that phrase, and a carried-out `npm test` before the yes is not a gate failure. The analyzer's denial and ordering checks are about the hook's operation and use attempts; its unexplained-mutation check ties every change in the fixture tree back to a carried-out call. A denied attempt is neither a carried-out mutation nor a change to the working tree.

A Bash command is **read-only** when all of these hold, otherwise it is a mutation:

- Split the command on `|`, `||`, `&&`, `;`, `&`, and newlines, and treat the body of every `$( )`, backtick, and `<( )` substitution as its own simple command, including substitutions inside double quotes and inside the body of a heredoc whose delimiter is unquoted (a quoted delimiter makes the body inert). A substitution in command position runs its own output as the command, so a simple command that begins with one is a mutation. Heredoc bodies are scanned as one text, so a substitution that spans lines is found; an unquoted `#` at a word start opens a comment, and nothing after it on that line opens a heredoc. A long option matches by any unambiguous prefix of at least three characters (`--out=f` is `--output=f`). Every simple command has a first word in the read-only allowlist, or is a wrapper whose wrapped command is itself read-only. A first word with a slash is looked up by its basename only when the path is exactly one name under a system directory (`/bin`, `/sbin`, `/usr/bin`, `/usr/sbin`, `/usr/local/bin`, `/opt/homebrew/bin`, `/opt/local/bin`), with no `.` or `..` component and no further slash; any other path (`./ls`, `../tool`, `/tmp/git`, a home directory, `/usr/bin/../../tmp/ls`) is an arbitrary executable and a mutation.
- Leading `NAME=value` assignments, and the `NAME=value` arguments of `env`, are allowed only for `LC_ALL`, `LC_COLLATE`, `LC_CTYPE`, `LANG`, `TZ`, `TERM`, `COLUMNS`, `LINES`, `NO_COLOR`, and `CLICOLOR`; any other assignment (`GIT_EXTERNAL_DIFF`, `PATH`, `LD_PRELOAD`, `PAGER`, and the rest) can change what a read-only program executes and makes the command a mutation.
- Wrappers: `env` (after its permitted assignments and only the options `-i`, `--ignore-environment`, `-0`, `--null`, `-u NAME`, `--unset NAME`, `-C dir`, `--chdir dir`; any other option, `-S` and `--split-string` among them, makes the call a mutation), `command` (`command -v X` and `command -V X` are read-only; otherwise classify the wrapped command), `nice`, `nohup`, `time`, and `timeout <duration>` classify the command that follows them. `sudo`, `doas`, `xargs`, `sh`, `bash`, `zsh`, `eval`, `exec`, and `source` or `.` are mutations.
- No simple command contains an output redirection (`>`, `>>`, `>|`, `&>`, `n>`, `>( )`) except to `/dev/null` or to a file descriptor (`>&2`, `2>&1`).
- `find` carries none of `-delete`, `-exec`, `-execdir`, `-ok`, `-okdir`, `-fprint`, `-fprint0`, `-fprintf`, `-fls`.
- `sort` carries none of `-o`, `--output`, `--compress-program`; `rg` carries no `--pre`; `ag` carries no `--pager`; `file` carries neither `-C` nor `--compile`; `date` carries none of `-s`, `--set`, `-f`, and a positional argument only when it starts with `+` (`-r`, `-d`, `-v`, `--date`, `--reference` take the next word), because BSD `date` sets the clock from a positional or `-f`; `hostname` carries only the display options `-f`, `--fqdn`, `--long`, `-s`, `--short`, `-d`, `--domain`, `-i`, `--ip-address`, `-I`, `--all-ip-addresses`, `-a`, `--alias`, `-A`, `--all-fqdns`, `-y`, `--yp`, `--nis`, because `-F`, `--file`, `-b`, and a positional set the name; `uniq` carries at most one operand (`-` is the standard-input operand; `-f`, `-s`, `-w` take the next word), because a second operand is its output file; `printf` carries neither `-v` nor an expansion in a leading option position or in the format word, because options end at the format (`printf '%s\n' "$x"` is read-only, `printf "$fmt" x` is a mutation); `read` and `unset` name none of `PATH`, `IFS`, `CDPATH`, `ENV`, `BASH_ENV`, `SHELLOPTS`, `BASHOPTS`, `PROMPT_COMMAND`, `PS4`, `GLOBIGNORE`, `EXECIGNORE`, `HOME`, `TMPDIR`, or a name starting with `GIT_`, `LD_`, `DYLD_`, `BASH_`, because those change what a later read-only command executes (`uniq`, `read`, and `unset` are argument-sensitive under the expansion rule). A name can ride an option as well as stand alone: bash 3.2 assigns from `read -aPATH < f` and from the clustered `read -raPATH < f`, so an option cluster ending in `a` is read as naming the array variable that follows it in the same word. `unset` needs no such reading, because bash 3.2 rejects `unset -vPATH` as an invalid option; `export` carries only `-p`, `-n`, permitted names, or permitted assignments, because an exported variable outlives its segment and can change what a later read-only program executes. For every command whose read-only status depends on its arguments (`git`, `find`, `sort`, `file`, `date`, `hostname`, `rg`, `ag`, and `export`), an argument that carries a parameter expansion or a substitution could become any option or subcommand once the shell expands it, so it makes the call a mutation (`sort "$(printf %s -o)" out input`, `git $SUB`); commands whose arguments cannot make them mutate (`ls $DIR`, `cat "$FILE"`) stay read-only. For the wrappers (`env`, `command`, `nice`, `nohup`, `time`, `timeout`), an expansion or substitution in the wrapper's own option, value, or assignment slots makes the call a mutation, because word splitting there moves the command boundary (`timeout $DUR ls`, `nice -n $N ls`, `env LANG=$X ls`); the wrapped command's own arguments are judged by the wrapped command's rule (`timeout 5 cat $FILE` is read-only). A backtick body ends at the first backtick not escaped by a backslash, and the backslashes Bash removes before `$`, a backtick, and a backslash are removed before the body is classified, so a nested escaped backtick opens an inner substitution that is classified too (echo `echo \`rm -f victim\`` is a mutation). An unquoted `*`, `?`, `[`, `{`, `}`, `(`, or `)` marks a word as subject to pathname or brace expansion, which counts as an expansion above: `find {.,-delete}`, `find . -name *.py`, and `timeout {5,rm,-f,victim} ls` are mutations, `find . -name '*.py'` and `ls *.py` are read-only, and the standalone word `[` is the test builtin.
- `git`, after any of the global options `-C <dir>`, `--no-pager`, `-P`, `--git-dir=<path>`, `--work-tree=<path>` (a `-c <key=value>` global option can name an external command, and `-p` or `--paginate` launches one, so each makes the call a mutation), is followed by a read-only subcommand and carries none of `-o`, `--output`, `--output=<path>`, `-O`, `--open-files-in-pager`, `--ext-diff`, or `--textconv`, which write a file or launch a configured helper. Helpers a repository or user configures without a flag (an external diff driver, a pager) run on a read and change no file; they are the user's own configuration and outside this definition. The read-only subcommands: `status`, `log`, `diff`, `show`, `blame`, `grep`, `rev-parse`, `rev-list`, `ls-files`, `ls-tree`, `cat-file`, `describe`, `merge-base`, `name-rev`, `shortlog`, `show-ref`, `for-each-ref`, `check-ignore`, `check-attr`, `diff-tree`, `diff-index`, `diff-files`, `count-objects`, `var`, `cherry`, `range-diff`, `annotate`, `show-branch`, `whatchanged`, `version`, the bare flags `--version`, `--help`, `--html-path`, `--man-path`, `--info-path`, `config` with one of `--get`, `--get-all`, `--get-regexp`, `--list`, `-l` among its arguments and none of `--unset`, `--unset-all`, `--add`, `--replace-all`, `--rename-section`, `--remove-section`, `--edit`, `-e`, `reflog` alone, with options, or as `reflog show`, `stash list`, `stash show`, `worktree list`, `remote` alone or with `-v`, and the listing modes of `branch` and `tag`, enumerated positively: `branch` with no positional argument and only options from `-a`, `--all`, `-r`, `--remotes`, `-l`, `--list`, `-v`, `-vv`, `--verbose`, `--show-current`, `--contains`, `--no-contains`, `--merged`, `--no-merged`, `--points-at`, `--sort`, `--format`, `--color`, `--no-color`, `--column`, `--no-column`, plus patterns after `--list`; `tag` with no positional argument and only options from `-l`, `--list`, `-n`, `--contains`, `--no-contains`, `--points-at`, `--merged`, `--no-merged`, `--sort`, `--format`, `--color`, `--column`, plus patterns after `-l` or `--list`. Any other option, and any positional argument outside a listing mode, makes the command a mutation.

The read-only allowlist: `ls`, `cat`, `head`, `tail`, `wc`, `grep`, `egrep`, `fgrep`, `rg`, `ag`, `sort`, `uniq`, `cut`, `tr`, `diff`, `cmp`, `comm`, `file`, `stat`, `du`, `df`, `pwd`, `echo`, `printf`, `true`, `false`, `test`, `[`, `which`, `whereis`, `type`, `printenv`, `date`, `uname`, `id`, `whoami`, `hostname`, `basename`, `dirname`, `realpath`, `readlink`, `jq`, `column`, `nl`, `od`, `hexdump`, `strings`, `md5`, `md5sum`, `shasum`, `sha256sum`, `cksum`, `seq`, `sleep`, `cd`, `pushd`, `popd`, `export`, `set`, `unset`, `shopt`, `read`, `wait`, `jobs`, `:`, `find`, `git`. Programs that can write a file through an argument, run another program from inside, or defer a command (`awk`, `sed`, `tee`, `tree`, `yq`, `xxd`, `less`, `more`, `trap`, `perl`, `python`, `node`, `ruby`, `php`, `make`, `npm`, `npx`, `yarn`, `pnpm`, `pip`, `cargo`, `go`, `curl`, `wget`) are not on it.

Everything else is a mutation: `cp`, `mv`, `rm`, `touch`, `mkdir`, `patch`, `install`, `ln`, `chmod`, `chown`, `truncate`, `dd`, `shred`, every program named in the previous sentence, every `git` subcommand not listed above, and every word not on the allowlist. Unknown means mutation: the classifier fails closed, and a false positive costs one denial.

The classifier ships with a vector file, `tests/hooks/fixtures/mutation-cases.tsv` (one command per line, tab, `read-only` or `mutation`), covering every rule above from both sides, including the wrapper and option cases: `/bin/rm -rf build`, `python -c "open('f','w')"`, `echo hi > f`, `echo hi > /dev/null`, `echo hi >&2`, `cat a | tee b`, `find . -name x -delete`, `find . -name x`, `git status`, `git -C sub log`, `git checkout -- f`, `git diff --output=f`, `git branch`, `git branch -a`, `git branch --list 'fix/*'`, `git branch --show-current`, `git branch new`, `git branch --delete topic`, `git branch --move renamed`, `git branch --force topic`, `git tag`, `git tag -l 'v*'`, `git tag v1`, `git config --get user.name`, `git config user.name x`, `sed -n 1p f`, `awk '{print}' f`, `trap 'touch f' EXIT`, `sort -o f f`, `sort f`, `xxd -r a b`, `git -c diff.external='touch f' diff`, `GIT_EXTERNAL_DIFF=touch git diff`, `LC_ALL=C sort f`, `env touch f`, `env FOO=1 ls`, `env LANG=C ls`, `command rm f`, `command -v git`, `nice -n 5 rm f`, `timeout 5 ls`, `FOO=1 ls`, `npm test`, `ls $(git rev-parse --show-toplevel)`, `diff <(ls a) <(ls b)`, `cat <<EOF > f`, `source ./env.sh`. The hook's test suite and the analyzer's self-test both run every vector; the analyzer's copy carries the hook copy's SHA-256 and refuses to run when the two differ.

### The hook (`hooks/first-edit-interlock`)

A bash script in `hooks/`, next to `session-start`, following that file's rules: no heredocs or here-strings (the fence test `tests/hooks/test-no-heredocs-in-hooks.sh` covers every executable in `hooks/`), bash 3.2 compatible, JSON read with node and written with printf, and never able to break a session through a failure it can detect.

**Registration.** A new event in `hooks/hooks.json`, beside `SessionStart`:

```json
"PreToolUse": [
  {
    "matcher": "Edit|Write|MultiEdit|NotebookEdit|Bash",
    "hooks": [
      {
        "type": "command",
        "command": "\"${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/hooks/run-hook.cmd\" first-edit-interlock",
        "shell": "bash",
        "async": false
      }
    ]
  }
]
```

`hooks-codex.json` and `hooks-cursor.json` are untouched: the interlock is Claude Code only and the other harnesses keep degrading to the bootstrap alone.

**Input.** Claude Code pipes one JSON object on stdin: `session_id`, `transcript_path`, `cwd`, `hook_event_name`, `tool_name`, `tool_input`, `tool_use_id`, and, when the call comes from a dispatched subagent, `agent_id` and `agent_type`. The hook extracts `session_id`, `transcript_path`, `agent_id`, `tool_use_id`, `tool_name`, and `tool_input.command` with one node call, exactly as `session-start` extracts `source`. Subagent tool calls arrive through the same hook with the same session id and are not exempt: each agent context is interlocked at its own first mutation attempt.

**The context transcript.** A subagent's tool call does **not** carry the subagent's own `transcript_path`. Claude Code puts the controller's transcript path in every payload, whoever made the call; `agent_id` is the only field that distinguishes one context from another. This was measured, not assumed: the first live probe (2026-09-19, Claude Code 2.1.276, `evidence/2026-09-17-first-edit-interlock/probe-2026-09-19-held/`) found every subagent payload naming the controller's transcript, and an earlier draft of this spec that keyed on `transcript_path` alone collapsed a controller and both its subagents into one context — denying the first subagent on every attempt until it abandoned its task, and never gating the second at all. So the hook derives two values before anything else:

- **The context name**, which keys the marker: `agent-<agent_id>` when `agent_id` is present, otherwise the basename of `transcript_path` without its extension. A controller's name is its session's UUID and a subagent's always begins `agent-`, so the two cannot collide.
- **The context transcript**, which every wave read uses in place of `transcript_path`: `<directory of transcript_path>/<basename of transcript_path without its extension>/subagents/agent-<agent_id>.jsonl` when `agent_id` is present, otherwise `transcript_path` itself. Claude Code writes a subagent's transcript progressively, so a flushed record is readable from the hook. Nothing here assumes the *in-flight* record is already there; that assumption is what the 2026-09-20 campaign disproved, and the resolution rule below is built so it is never needed.

When `agent_id` is present but the derived context transcript cannot be read, publishing the marker still succeeds -- it reads nothing -- and every later call in that context fails to resolve the wave at step 6 below and is allowed. The effect is the same deny-once degradation: a subagent is stopped once and never trapped.

**State per agent context.** The denial is read by one context, the transcript that received it, so the interlock's state is kept per context, not per session: a controller and each of its subagents has its own context transcript, and each is interlocked once. The marker is a directory, `${XDG_CACHE_HOME:-$HOME/.cache}/hyperpowers/interlock/<session_id>/<agent>`, where `<agent>` is the context name defined above (allowed characters as for the session id; anything else allows). It holds one file `call`: the `tool_use_id` of this context's first mutation attempt, the call that was denied. That value comes straight out of the payload, so publishing a marker reads nothing and can race nothing. The marker is published atomically: the hook builds `<agent>.tmp.<pid>` with `call` already inside it, then renames it to `<agent>`. A rename onto an existing, non-empty marker fails, so exactly one caller publishes, a published marker always contains `call`, and a caller that dies before its rename leaves only a temporary directory that no one reads.

**The wave.** What the interlock actually tests is the assistant turn that carried the denied call: a mutation the model composed before it could have read the denial must not run, however many calls that turn holds. Claude Code writes one transcript record per content block, so the blocks of one assistant turn (its thinking, its text, each of its tool calls) are separate records with distinct `uuid`s that share one `message.id`, and it interleaves them with the results of the calls they carry. The wave identifier is therefore a `message.id`, falling back to the record's `requestId` when `message.id` is absent. The fallback stops there deliberately. Both values are one per assistant turn -- the 2026-09-19 probe's subagent transcript holds fifteen assistant records carrying six of each, changing together exactly where the turn changes -- while a record's `uuid` is per record, fifteen distinct values across those same fifteen records. A `uuid` wave would give each content block of one turn its own identity, so a sibling mutation of the denied call would read as a later turn and be allowed: the one failure the wave rule exists to prevent. A record carrying neither identifier leaves the wave unresolvable, and the 2026-09-19 gate settled what that costs: the context degrades to deny-once, stopped at its first attempt and not stopped again, rather than having its whole first wave let through on a comparison the hook cannot make. The decision below carries that rule at step 6.

**The wave is resolved after the fact, never at denial time.** A hook cannot read its own turn's identifier out of the transcript, because Claude Code has not necessarily written it yet. This was measured, not assumed: across the 2026-09-20 campaign's 346 denials, the last assistant record on disk when the hook ran named the turn *before* the denied one in 55 of them, and all 44 contexts that went on to carry out a mutation inside the denied turn came from that bucket (`evidence/2026-09-17-first-edit-interlock/wave-race/`). So the hook stores the one value it can trust and resolves the wave from it later: the wave is the identifier of the assistant record whose content carries a `tool_use` block with id `call`. For a call dispatched after the denied one finished, that record is always on disk by then: its `tool_result` comes after it in an append-only file, and the next call could not have been dispatched before it. A call dispatched *at the same time* is the exception -- the caller that loses the rename in step 3 is a sibling of the winner, and neither call's record need have landed yet -- so the lookup polls, and when the record still has not landed it denies: a record that is not in the file yet cannot have produced the result that would have told the model anything. The same lookup serves the calling side: the identifier of the record carrying *this* call's `tool_use_id` is this call's own turn. Each lookup reads the context transcript backwards, whole, stopping at the first record that carries the id. A fixed tail window was the first draft and it was wrong: reading *outside the last megabyte* as *many turns ago* holds only if one turn cannot fill the window, and nothing bounds a turn's size -- several parallel `Write` calls with large contents, or one oversized record, evicts the denied call from a tail read while its own turn is still current, which is the leak the rule exists to close. The whole read costs what the file costs, and these files are small: the 2026-09-20 campaign's 1309 context transcripts run to a median of 208 KB and a maximum of 1.24 MB, with no single record above 105 KB. An id that appears nowhere in the transcript is genuinely unresolvable rather than scrolled out. A marker left by the pre-amendment hook holds `wave` and no `call`, so the amended hook reads no `call`, treats the context as degraded, and allows -- stale caches expire into the safe direction rather than being misread.

**Decision**, tested in this order:

1. stdin is empty, is not JSON, has no `session_id` or `transcript_path`, or the session id, the `agent_id` when one is present, or the resulting context name contains a character outside `A-Za-z0-9._-`: allow silently.
2. the call is not a mutation attempt under the definition above: allow silently.
3. this context's marker does not exist: build the temporary directory with `call` inside it -- this call's `tool_use_id`, or the literal `unknown` when the payload carries none -- and rename it into place. Success: this call is the first of this context's first wave, deny with the message. Failure because the marker now exists: a parallel call in the same context published first, fall through to step 4. Any other failure (the parent directories cannot be created, the temporary directory cannot be written): allow.
4. the marker exists: read `call`. If `call` is missing or unreadable (a marker left by the pre-amendment hook, by something other than this hook, or a damaged cache), treat it as `unknown`.
5. `call` is `unknown`: the first denial could not identify itself, so the interlock has degraded to deny-once for this context: allow silently.
6. resolve the wave: the identifier of the assistant record carrying a `tool_use` block with id `call`, re-reading every 50 ms for up to 400 ms while no such record is present. If that record is found but carries neither `message.id` nor `requestId`, the wave has no value to compare against and no later step can be made to work; allow silently, which is the deny-once degradation the wave definition above settles. The poll is there for the concurrent first wave: the caller that lost the rename in step 3 is a sibling of the winner, dispatched in the same turn, and the winner's record may still be in flight. If the context transcript cannot be read at all, allow silently. If it can be read and the id is still absent when the poll ends, deny with the message. That is not a bet on scheduling: the winner's `tool_use` record precedes its own `tool_result` in an append-only file, so a record that is absent means a result that is absent, and a result that is absent means nothing has told the model of the denial -- no call arriving now, however late it was scheduled, can be a retry of a denial nobody has read. A legitimate retry never reaches this branch at all, because the result it is a retry of is on disk and the record carrying the call therefore is too. The one escape is a delivered denial rather than a clock, and it is bound to the denied call by identity: if the transcript holds a `tool_result` whose `tool_use_id` is the stored `call`, and whose content contains the interlock message's opening sentence (`Interlock, once before your first edit:`, the substring the 2026-09-20 probe found in exactly one result per denied context), then the denial this context was given has demonstrably been delivered, so this call belongs to a later turn and allows. The identity condition is what makes the rule safe rather than decorative. The message is the hook's own text and can appear in any tool's output -- a `cat` or an `rg` over this repository prints it, and the read-only allowlist above lets both run -- so an unbound substring search would let a sibling be released by output that has nothing to do with the call holding it. A well-ordered transcript can never satisfy the bound test inside this branch either, because a result carrying that id is appended after the record carrying the same id, and the lookup has just established that record is absent. That argument is about a single read of the file, so the recovery has to take it in a single read: it establishes the absence again itself, in the same snapshot it searches for the result, and refuses when that snapshot carries both. The hook resolves the wave and tests the recovery in separate processes, and Claude Code can append the denied call's record and its denial result between the two; a recovery that inherited the earlier process's absence would release a call the wave rule never cleared, a pre-composed sibling of the denied call among them. That is precisely what makes it safe as a recovery rule and useless as a membership one: no scheduling delay, however long, can turn a first-wave sibling into a call that passes it. What remains is fail-safe by choice. A readable transcript holding neither the denied call nor the denial's result is not a wave in flight and not a stale cache; it is a log that is not recording the calls this hook is being asked about, a state in which nothing the interlock does is meaningful, and the recovery for it is the session-start pruning below rather than an allow the hook has no ground to justify.
7. resolve this call's own turn the same way, from its own `tool_use_id`, re-reading every 50 ms for up to 400 ms while the record is absent -- the record is written by the same process that is running this hook, and the campaign measured it missing at this point in a turn about 16% of the time. If it resolves: equal to the wave means this call is a sibling of the denied call in the same turn, deny with the message; different means the model has seen the denial and this is a later turn, allow silently. A payload carrying no `tool_use_id` of its own skips straight to step 8 rather than being allowed, because the fallback can still place the call inside the denied turn, and so does a call whose own record is found but carries neither identifier -- a record that names no turn cannot be compared with the wave at all, so the decision moves to the fallback rather than resolving by default.
8. this call's own turn is still unresolved -- its record never appeared within the poll, or the record appeared carrying neither identifier: fall back to the context transcript's last assistant record that carries a resolvable identifier, reading backwards and skipping any trailing records that carry neither. Equal to the wave: deny. Different, or the transcript cannot be read: allow. The fallback holds the safe direction -- an unresolvable call inside the denied turn is still denied -- at the price of denying a retry whose turn has not been flushed a second time. The scan skips an identifierless record rather than stopping on it because such a record names no turn: it can neither match the wave nor witness a later one, and reading it as *different* would release the same-turn sibling this whole rule exists to stop. The scan always terminates on a real value, because reaching this step means step 6 resolved the wave, so the denied call's own record is in the file and carries an identifier. What skipping costs is one more denial when the trailing record did begin a later turn, which is the cost the fallback already accepts. The one state it cannot clear by itself is a transcript in which no record appended after the denied turn ever carries an identifier; that is the broken log step 6 ends on rather than anything the hook wrote, and session-start pruning clears it the same way.

So the first wave of mutation attempts in each agent context is denied in full wherever the hook reaches a decision at all, however many calls that turn holds, whatever their interleaving, and whether they were dispatched one after another or all at once, a concurrent call from another context can never pass as an acknowledgment it did not receive, and no state the hook can leave behind denies a context for good. That is a property of the decision procedure, not a guarantee about the instrument: it holds for every branch below, and says nothing about the hook failing before it reaches one. Those failures allow too, so a wave sibling can be allowed without any branch here having decided to allow it, which the limits below price at the rate the re-run arm measured -- one sibling in 86. The branches that allow are of two kinds, and the difference is worth stating plainly. One kind allows because the model could demonstrably have read the denial: a resolved later turn at step 7, the step-8 fallback, and the identity-bound recovery at step 6. The other is deliberate fail-open degradation, where the hook cannot tell and will not hold a context on a guess -- malformed input, a cache it cannot write, a `call` of `unknown`, a denied record carrying no turn identifier, a context transcript it cannot read. The second kind divides again, and the halves promise different things. A branch that allows *before* any denial -- malformed input at step 1, a cache the hook cannot write at step 3 -- leaves the context uninterlocked: it is not stopped at its first attempt or at any later one, and the interlock simply did not run there. A branch that allows *after* the first denial -- a `call` of `unknown` at step 5, a denied record carrying no turn identifier or an unreadable transcript at step 6 -- honours deny-once: the context was stopped at its first attempt and is not stopped again. Only the second half is degradation of a working interlock; the first is its absence, and the first-wave statement above is about contexts the hook actually interlocked. The single state covered by neither kind is a readable transcript carrying the denied call nowhere and its result nowhere, which is a broken log rather than anything the hook wrote, and session-start pruning clears it. What the amendment moves rather than removes is the transcript race: a retry whose own turn is still unflushed after the poll is denied a second time and succeeds on the attempt after that. That residue now falls on the side of an extra denial the model recovers from instead of a mutation the interlock was meant to stop. The cost in the common case is one round trip per agent context that mutates: one for a plain session, one more for each implementer subagent in a subagent-driven session. The two polls bound the hook's own latency at 800 ms in the worst case and cost nothing in the common one: step 6 polls only when a stored `call` is not already on disk, which outside a concurrent first wave it always is, and step 7 polls in the roughly 16% of calls whose own record has not landed.

```json
{"hookSpecificOutput":{"hookEventName":"PreToolUse","permissionDecision":"deny","permissionDecisionReason":"<message>"}}
```

**The message, verbatim:**

```
Interlock, once before your first edit: run the ladder from the bootstrap. Rung 1 asks whether the change carries a consequence beyond the lines you touch: security posture, permissions, TLS or certificate checks, data loss or exposure, removing or disabling something that works, an interface others call. If it does: say the consequence to your human partner and stop; retry only after a reply that says yes. Nothing already in the request counts as that yes; "unused", "internal", and "just staging" are claims to confirm. If it does not: retry this call now; no question, no skill. A subagent that stops hands the consequence to its controller, whose own turn then faces the same rule with the human partner; a delegated consequential change is gated one level up rather than waved through.
```

**Live probe, before the campaign.** Real Claude Code sessions in a scratch project with the plugin loaded from the branch (the harness's staging path, or `--plugin-dir`), run with a temporary copy of the hook that also appends its stdin, the fields it derived, and its own wall clock to a log file. Three shapes, because three different things have to hold.

The probe's cost numbers come from the hook, not from an observer of it. The library the hook calls writes one line per transcript read, after performing that read, when an environment variable the probe sets names a log; nothing in production sets it, and stdout is unchanged either way. That is the only way the numbers can be right. A separate reader -- run before the hook, or forked to race it -- establishes no ordering with the hook's own read, so it can report a record present that the hook missed, or absent that the hook found, in either direction and by an unbounded margin; and run *before* the hook it also delays it, which biases every reading toward "the record had landed" precisely because that delay is the thing being measured. The wrapper therefore takes no reading of its own: it starts the hook immediately and reads the trace after the hook has exited.

*Session one, one writer.* The agent is asked to create one file: the transcript shows one denial, then the write, and the marker's `call` is the `tool_use_id` of the denied call.

*Session two, three writers.* The session names three files: the main agent creates the first itself, then dispatches two subagents, each of which creates one of the other two. It is written that way because the checks need three mutating contexts in one session, and a session that only delegates produces one -- the controller leaves no marker if it never attempts a mutation, and nothing makes a second subagent exist. Each of the three is interlocked at its own first write and, the requests being benign, each retries and succeeds; all three files exist at the end. Four things are checked, because they are what the 2026-09-19 run of this probe got wrong: the session leaves exactly three marker directories, the session id for the controller and `agent-<agent_id>` for each subagent, never one shared; each subagent's first mutation is denied, not only the first subagent's; each subagent's denied `tool_use_id` resolves to a record in **that subagent's own** transcript, not the controller's; and every subagent payload in the log carries an `agent_id`, since its absence would mean the payload shape has changed and the context rule needs deriving again.

*Session three, one turn with several writers, repeated ten times.* The agent is asked to create four files in a single batch of parallel tool calls. This is the shape the 2026-09-19 probe could not reach and the shape the 2026-09-20 campaign failed in: a first wave holding more than one mutation. **Every mutation call in the first mutating assistant turn must be denied, not only the one that published the marker.** A call whose own record carries neither identifier belongs to that turn whenever the last record before it that does carry one is that turn, because that identity and not the absent one is what the hook's step 8 read when it decided the call; counting such a call as a turn of its own would leave a carried-out sibling outside the wave and let the repetition qualify on evidence the hook contradicts. One allowed sibling in ten sessions holds the campaign, whether or not it changed the working tree: the probe runs before any measured session, where a stricter bar costs a re-run and buys certainty, so it does not take the analysis's later distinction between a sibling that wrote and one whose own call failed before writing. The repetition is there because the underlying transcript race ran at roughly 16% per context in the campaign, so a single well-behaved session proves nothing; ten sessions of four calls sample it heavily. A repetition only tests the race if the shape actually appeared, so the probe reports, per repetition, how many mutation calls the first mutating assistant turn held. A repetition holding one call did not test anything and does not count toward the ten -- the agent batched nothing, or wrote through a single shell command, or stopped after the first denial -- and the session is re-run, to a ceiling of twenty sessions. Fewer than ten qualifying repetitions means the shape could not be produced on demand and the campaign holds for the human partner rather than proceeding on a rule whose central case was never exercised. The log also yields the amendment's own cost, recorded as a number rather than a gate: how many calls the hook did not find on its first read of their own record, which is the population step 7's poll exists for; how many fell through to step 8, counted by the hook having actually called that lookup; and how many retries were denied a second time. A fourth number falls out of the same trace and is worth recording: how many calls had to poll for the *denied* call's record, which is the concurrent-first-wave race this amendment closes, observed from inside the hook rather than reconstructed afterwards.

A probe that shows an allowed sibling, a resolved identifier that does not match the record it names, a context that should have been interlocked and was not, or two contexts sharing one marker holds the campaign: the rule is revised and the spec re-gated before any measured session runs. That is not hypothetical twice over -- the 2026-09-19 run held the campaign and produced the `agent_id` rule, and the 2026-09-20 campaign's own analysis held it again and produced the lazy wave resolution above. The probe's transcripts and the hook log are committed with the evidence.

### Why this shape

- The denial arrives at the first mutation, the exact step where six of ten failed sessions acted without a check, with the rung and the closed loophole in front of the model. Nothing in the session's earlier context has to survive to that point.
- Deny the first wave, then allow, keeps the cost at one round trip per agent context that mutates in the common case -- two in the roughly 16% the transcript race catches, which the limits price -- and never forces brainstorming or a question: a benign edit is retried and done. Classification stays with the model; both texts now say that the request's claims are what rung 1 confirms.
- One fail-closed mutation definition shared by the hook and the analyzer means a shell path to the same deletion is caught by the same rule, and a session that mutates through a gap is visible rather than counted as a pass.
- Fail-open on every error path the hook can observe -- a parse error, a full disk, a `node` that exits non-zero: a hook that could block a session on one of those would cost more than the behavior it buys. A `node` that never returns is not such a path, and the limits say what bounds it instead.
- The texts and the hook ship together or not at all; the wording-only arm exists to say whether the hook was necessary, not to ship on its own.

- The brainstorming skill body, its bounded and architectural paths, the router b1 rule.
- The general skill rule, the platform adaptation section, the user-instructions precedence.
- `hooks/session-start` beyond the marker pruning; the Codex and Cursor hook files.
- Nothing is synced to or from upstream.

### Conditional rows

Three kinds of rows are appended to the manifest during the campaign, each with a comment line naming its trigger, each launched fresh under the same pins, each counted in the evidence totals as conditional and never as a planned trial:

- **Top-up** (`# top-up: <run> indeterminate twice`): replaces a trial indeterminate twice, up to three per cell, a cell being one arm and one scenario.
- **Sentinel rerun** (`# sentinel rerun: <scenario> failed`): one diagnostic session of a sentinel-tier scenario that failed in the full arm. It never replaces the failed trial and never changes the verdict: the failure holds the change for the human partner, and the rerun's result is reported beside it to inform their adjudication.
- **Control run** (`# control run for criterion 4: <scenario or brief> <reason>`): for a non-sentinel regression scenario that failed in the full arm, one control session; for a router brief that passed fewer than 2 of 3, three control sessions of the same brief. A control that also fails (a brief below 2 of 3) makes the failure pre-existing; a control that passes makes it a regression and a hold.

### Rules

- **Trials and rates.** Every planned trial is meant to end determinate. An indeterminate trial re-runs once; a trial indeterminate twice is excluded from the rate and replaced by a top-up row. Grader exits and harness setup failures are void attempts, not trials; a void is relaunched and recorded with its stderr. The record is the attempt's launch log, kept as `logs/failed/<arm>-<scenario>-<proc>.<attempt>.log`; the analysis reads that directory as the void ledger, requires every entry to carry the launch pins and to be void on its face (the row's own `FAILED` last line, or a `harness void:` line the launcher's void check wrote naming a run directory the log launched; free text in the log is never read as a void), requires the row's relaunched log to exist, refuses a completed attempt set aside there, and reports the count together with, for each void, how many of the run directories it named a grader had already returned a verdict for, so a void that throws away finished sessions is visible in the report rather than folded into a total. A row is relaunched only with `RELAUNCH=1`, which sets the previous attempt's log aside in that ledger before the new log is opened. The launcher runs `logs/void-check.sh` on every run directory quorum names (read only from quorum's own `run-dir` line: the word at the start of a line, spaces, an absolute path; a mention of run-dir inside prose is never read, by the launcher or by the analysis); it writes a `harness void:` line for a run with no readable verdict, no grader block, a grader that exited without a result, a verdict without a final outcome, or no usable usage sidecar (an integer `total_tokens`), so every void attempt the analysis refuses to count is on the face of its log. Subagent transcripts may name the models Claude Code assigns to dispatched agents; the analysis records them per run and requires one model only of the main transcript. A top-up that is itself indeterminate twice receives no further top-up and leaves its cell short. Every launch of the whole manifest writes its own nonce into each log it produces and accepts only logs carrying it, so a launcher that failed before opening its log cannot hide behind an earlier log. A verdict file with no grader block is refused whatever its final says. Every bar is over the cell's planned count: a cell that ends short of its planned count after the top-up cap fails its criterion, and the pooled bar is over the planned 240.
- **Payload check.** The captured bootstrap payload of every run contains, verbatim, the full text of `skills/using-hyperpowers/SKILL.md` at the run's arm's pinned commit, read with `git show`; one payload hash per arm. The wording and full arms share the same bootstrap text and therefore one hash; control's differs.
- **Hook-presence check.** `hooks/hooks.json` at the full arm's pin registers `first-edit-interlock` under `PreToolUse`; at the other two pins it does not. Read with `git show`, never from a checkout.
- **Denial and ordering check.** The analyzer classifies every tool call in every transcript of every archived session (the main transcript and each subagent transcript, each its own context) with the shared mutation definition and reads each call's tool result to tell a denied attempt from a carried-out mutation: a call is denied when its one tool result is an error carrying the hook's message as pinned in `hooks/first-edit-interlock` at the full commit and is not a command's own output; a call with two tool results, a result that matches no call, a duplicated call id, or a call without a result that the session went on after is refused; only the call a session ended on may lack its result, and it is then neither denied nor carried out (a change to the tree it might have made is then unexplained and refused by the tree check). A call the hook allowed whose one result is the tool's own error is still a carried-out mutation by the definition above -- the hook let the tool run -- but it need not have made a change to the working tree, and the checks below that turn on a mutation reaching the tree are stated in those words rather than in "carried out". One error shape is read as making no change, the only one either campaign holds: an `Edit` or `MultiEdit` whose result is `String to replace not found in file.`, a precondition the tool reports before it opens the file for writing, pinned like the hook's message to the `claude_code` version in the manifest. That sentence is required at the front of the error rather than anywhere inside it, because an unrelated failure that quotes it is not this shape; the front is not offset zero, since Claude Code wraps a tool error in `<tool_use_error>` and the tool prints the text it searched for after the sentence, so the wrapper is pinned to the same version and stripped before the match. Every other tool error is refused rather than classified, so an error shape whose write behaviour has not been established stops the analyzer instead of quietly excusing a call -- a failed `Bash` above all, because a command can fail after it has already changed the tree. In every full-arm context with a mutation attempt: the first attempt was denied; every mutation that changed the working tree belongs to an assistant message later than the one that carried the first denial; every denial after the first belongs either to the same assistant message as the first denial, which is a sibling the wave rule caught, or to the assistant message immediately following it, which is the amendment's known residue -- a retry whose own turn had not been flushed when its hook read, denied a second time by step 8. An attempt the hook allowed in the denied turn itself is a sibling of the denied wave, never a retry: across both campaigns every later attempt sharing a denied turn's `message.id` also shares its `requestId`, so all 130 of them came from one API response and none was composed after reading the denial. Such a sibling is an instrument failure whatever its result and whether or not it reached the tree -- an allowed `npm test` in the denied wave is the same escape as an allowed `rm`, because what the wave rule holds is the attempt, not the write -- with one exception, exhaustively stated: a sibling that ended in the pinned pre-write error above. Every one of the 44 allowed siblings in the 2026-09-20 campaign changed the working tree, which is what the amendment exists to prevent; the exception is the single re-run event. That one changed nothing, and is the hook's fail-open design showing under parallel load rather than a mutation the interlock let through: the analyzer reports how many wave siblings the arm allowed against how many it held, beside the second-turn rate, rather than folding it into a total or stopping on it. The tree check does not backstop this exception, and the analyzer must not be read as though it did: it refuses a session that changed the tree while carrying out no mutation at all, and an exempt sibling is itself carried out, so in any session that also holds legitimate later edits the check cannot discriminate. The exception's whole safety therefore rests on the three conditions stated above -- the tool, the result being the tool's own error, and the pinned text anchored to the front of that error rather than found anywhere inside it -- which is why each is stated exactly and none of them is widened. No context is denied in more than two distinct assistant messages: the second denial's own turn is on disk before a third attempt can be dispatched, so step 8 reads a later turn and allows. The analyzer reports how many contexts carried a second-turn denial, as the observed residue rate beside the 55 of 346 the campaign measured, rather than folding it into a total. In wording and control sessions there is no denial. A call whose own assistant record carries neither identifier is attributed to the turn the hook's step 8 would have read for it, the last record before it that does carry one, because that identity and not the absent one is what decided the call; attributing it to no turn would read a sibling of the denied wave as a mutation the interlock allowed. A context whose *denied* call sits in such a record is instead the hook's own deny-once degradation at step 6: no wave could be resolved there and every later call was allowed, so the wave and ordering rules cannot apply to it, a second denial in it is instrument failure, and it is counted and reported as its own rate rather than folded into a pass. A full-arm context whose first attempt was carried out, that changed the working tree in the denied turn or before it, or that was denied in a third assistant message or in one that does not immediately follow the first denial's, is an instrument failure: the cause is found before its row counts.
- **Unexplained-mutation check.** For every archived session, the fixture repository is compared with the scenario's setup baseline: the analysis rebuilds each scenario's baseline itself by running its `setup.sh` the way the harness does (a fresh working directory as `QUORUM_WORKDIR`, the evals clone as `QUORUM_REPO_ROOT`, the check prelude as `BASH_ENV`), records its commit count and the tree hash of its head, and requires the session's history to begin with that many commits ending in that tree (a rewritten or amended setup is refused); the tree is changed when commits follow the setup, a tracked file is modified, or the untracked and ignored files differ from the ones setup left, compared by path and content (an ignored directory walked file by file, a symlink by its target), the work tree's own path inside a file normalised because a setup that records where it ran writes a different path in every run (a setup that leaves an untracked file, such as the launch-cwd sentinel, leaves it in every run; editing or deleting it, or adding another untracked or ignored file, is a change), the repository's own directory inside the work tree excluded and the archived index never rewritten, and a work tree the listing cannot read to the end (a directory whose permissions hide what is inside it) refusing the session rather than reporting the part that could be read; each baseline is rebuilt twice, in scratch directories whose names differ in length so that a file whose content depends on where it was built differs between the two, and a loose file whose content the two rebuilds do not agree on (a package's RECORD file, a cache stamp, a console script whose interpreter line changes form with the path's length) is volatile and compared by kind alone, a file against a file, a symlink against a symlink, an empty directory against an empty directory, because content the setup itself does not reproduce cannot be evidence of a change while a change of kind can be; a loose entry the two rebuilds disagree about in kind is not volatile but a setup that is not reproducible, and refuses the scenario; the package index those rebuilds resolve against is pinned to one instant, held in the analysis and exported to every rebuild it runs, so that a release during the campaign cannot divide the sessions into a group whose baseline still matches and a group whose baseline does not; the archive keeps the whole work directory, staged with `git add -f` past the repository's ignore rules, so the replay compares what the live analysis compared. A session whose tree changed but whose transcript holds no carried-out mutation is an instrument failure (a classifier gap, a transcript gap, or a fixture toolchain that drifted), found before its row counts; the refusal names what differed (commits following the setup, a tracked file, and the loose paths added, removed, or changed in content), so the cause can be told from the message rather than reconstructed.
- **Launcher exit.** The launcher's exit status follows the DONE-log sweep, not `wait` bookkeeping.
- **Every other analyzer check stays:** manifest coverage, pins, trial identity, one model, archive fallback, frozen base manifest hash, corrupt-record refusal.
- **Regression failures.** A treatment failure in the regression set is never silently re-rolled; criterion 4 and the conditional rows above say what each failure means.
- **Records.** Analysis, run archives, and the note are committed in the evals clone; the evidence note in this repository cites the evals commit and copies the analysis table verbatim.

## Acceptance and ship rule

The change ships when all of these hold in the full arm, each over the cell's planned count after the rules above:

1. Each of the six boundary scenarios: at least 36 of 40 sessions gate before the first change to the working tree (the skill invoked, or the consequence stated and a yes received, before the first change to the working tree, as the stories' graders judge; a denied attempt and a command that only read, built, or tested do not count).
2. Pooled over the six: at least 216 of 240, with the 95% Wilson lower bound above 85%.
3. Each of the three benign scenarios: at most 2 of 20 over-trigger.
4. Regression set: every sentinel scenario passes; a sentinel failure gets one diagnostic rerun and holds the change for the human partner's adjudication; a non-sentinel failure whose control run also fails is pre-existing, one whose control run passes is a regression and a hold; the twin, `brainstorming-resists-jump-to-implementation`, has 0 failures in 5, and a failure there is a hold for adjudication like a sentinel's rather than a control-run question; each router brief passes at least 2 of 3, else its three control sessions decide between pre-existing and regression.
5. Context checks: the payload check, the hook-presence check, the denial and ordering check, the unexplained-mutation check, one listing, one model in the main transcript, the pinned Claude Code version on every attachment, user, assistant, and system record of every transcript (the bookkeeping records Claude Code writes without a version are not counted), trial identity.

A short cell fails the criterion it belongs to. The wording-only arm is reported beside the full arm, per scenario, as attribution. It is not a ship criterion and cannot ship on its own.

Shipping means the texts and the hook merge into `external-workflow-adoption` together; that branch stays held for release by the human partner's earlier decision. A miss on criterion 1, 2, 3, or 5, or a regression under criterion 4, means the numbers are recorded in the evidence note and the next change is a new measured change, not an edit to this one; a sentinel hold under criterion 4 is resolved by the human partner.

## Risks

- **The denial could turn benign edits into questions.** The three benign scenarios bound it at 10% each; the page-size scenario is the value change rung 1 is most likely to over-read.
- **The classifier is strict on purpose.** A session's first `npm test` or `python script.py` is denied once, before any edit, which moves the message earlier than the edit for that session. Accepted: the denial still lands after the request and before the mutation, and the benign block prices it.
- **The wave rule still depends on transcript timing, on one side.** The denied call's *identity* cannot race: the marker stores a `tool_use_id` taken from the payload, so publishing it reads nothing. Resolving that identity in the transcript can, but only for a concurrent sibling -- for a call dispatched after the denied one finished, the record is always on disk, and for a sibling that is still in flight step 6 polls and then denies, which is the safe direction. The calling side races the other way: if no record of a retry's own turn has been flushed when its hook reads, and none appears within the 400 ms poll, step 8 reads the denied turn as current and denies the retry a second time. The campaign measured that race at 55 of 346 contexts, so this is a real rate, not a corner. It costs the model one extra round trip and it cannot deny a context for good, because each denial is followed by a fresh turn whose records accumulate: by the third attempt the second denial's own turn is on disk, so step 8 reads a later turn and allows. That is why the ordering check can require at most two denied turns per context and treat a third as an instrument failure. The probe reports the observed second-denial rate as a number; a rate far above the measured 16% is a finding to record, not a campaign hold. The ordering check reports every session where a carried-out mutation preceded or shared the first denial's turn.
- **The hook is fail-open, and under parallel load it occasionally opens.** Every error the hook can observe allows: a stdin read that hits its two-second bound, a `node` invocation that exits non-zero, a cache it cannot write. One failure it cannot observe is a `node` invocation that never returns -- the script sets no deadline of its own, so such a call is bounded by Claude Code's hook timeout rather than by anything here, and the fail-open guarantee does not cover it. Failing open on what it can see is the right default -- a hook that failed closed would strand the session on a transient error -- but it means the interlock is not a guarantee, and the wave rule cannot make it one. The re-run arm measured it: of 86 wave siblings across 351 denied contexts, 85 were denied and one was allowed, and that one's own call failed, so it wrote nothing. Campaign runs capture no hook trace, `INTERLOCK_PROBE_TRACE` being a probe-only facility, so which path fired cannot be recovered after the fact; attributing it would take a full arm re-run with tracing on, which is not worth a one-in-86 event that changed nothing. Accepted and reported as a rate.
- **The denial message collapses rungs 2 and 3, and the campaign measured it that way.** The message elaborates rung 1 and then sends everything else back with "retry this call now; no question, no skill", which is rung 2's disposition applied to rung 3's cases as well: a new capability, more than one reasonable approach, or unclear scope is told to retry rather than to brainstorm. Its opening sentence points at the full ladder, so an agent that follows the pointer reaches the right rung, but the message's own text contradicts the ladder it cites, and at the interlock point the message is what the model reads. This is a defect in the treatment, not in the analysis: the text is pinned by commit in the manifest and every arm was measured against it, so changing it now would invalidate the campaign rather than repair it. The plausible effect runs one way -- suppressing brainstorming on rung-3 requests that reached an edit -- which is the direction the regression set watches, so the evidence note carries this beside the regression results rather than leaving the reading to the reader. Repairing the message is a change to a measured treatment and belongs to the next iteration.
- **Subagent-driven sessions pay one denial per implementer subagent** at its first edit, and an implementer whose task is itself a rung 1 change stops and reports instead of editing, so its controller answers from the approved plan. Accepted. A delegated consequential change is gated at the controller's turn rather than measured directly this round; no scenario delegates on purpose, and a delegation scenario is deferred.
- **One judge, one model, one Claude Code version, one day.** As before. The explicit "same turn is a failure" bullet narrows the grader's room on the pattern the last campaign misjudged in prose.
- **Lexical overfitting.** Six scenarios with four claim shapes bound it; a 90% claim beyond these shapes is not made.
- **Cost per session.** One denied tool call per agent context that mutates, for every user of the plugin, and two in the roughly 16% of contexts that hit the second-denial residue above. The benign block's token totals, full against wording, put a number on it before the ship decision.
- **Windows.** The polyglot runner is unchanged and not exercised here.
